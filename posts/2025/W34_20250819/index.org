** ostentatious
[ /ˌästənˈtāshəs/ ]
(adj.) Ostentatious describes someone or something that displays wealth, knowledge, power, etc., in a way that is meant to attract attention, admiration, or envy. Things that are ostentatious tend to stand out as overly elaborate or conspicuous.

The resort town is famous for its extravagantly expensive summer homes, which some tourists view as impressive and others merely ostentatious.


** ductile
[ /ˈdəktəl/ ]
(adj.) capable of being drawn out into wire or thread; easily led or influenced; capable of being fashioned into a new form.

The web-like blue and gold dial is housed in a 40mm case made of tantalum, a very hard, ductile, lustrous, blue-gray transition metal that is highly corrosion-resistant.
例句中的 dail 在这里的含义是表盘。


** synonym
[ /ˈsinəˌnim/ ]
(n.) one of two or more words or expressions of the same language that have the same or nearly the same meaning in some or all senses.

The debris hurtled by so fast that the New York Times editorial page seemed to run out of synonyms for disgust, revulsion and abuse.


** corrosion
[ /kəˈrōzhən/ ]
(n.) the action, process, or effect of corroding

The corrosion of family values that is often brought on by great wealth.


** lustrous
[ /ˈləstrəs/ ]
(adj.) reflecting light evenly and efficiently without glitter or sparkle

The lustrous finish on the satin bedspread adds to the feeling of luxury.


** debris
[ /dəˈbrē/ ]
(n.) the remains of something broken down or destroyed.

After the earthquake, rescuers began digging through the debris in search of survivors.


** extravagantly
[ /ikˈstravigənt/ ]
(adj.) exceeding the limits of reason or necessity

The company has been making extravagant claims about the drug's effectiveness.


** conspicuous
[ /kənˈspikyəwəs/ ]
(adj.) obvious to the eye or mind

There were a number of conspicuous changes to the building.


** prominent
[ /ˈprämənənt/ ]
(adj.) standing out or projecting beyond a surface or line

He quickly became prominent in the music industry.


** leverage
[ /ˈlevərij/ ]
(n.) the action of a lever or the mechanical advantage gained by it
(vt.) to provide (something, such as a corporation) or supplement (something, such as money) with leverage

The union's size gave it leverage in the labor contract negotiations.
The company wants to leverage its brands more effectively.

    1 ostentatious

    [ ˌästənˈtāshəs ]
    (adj.) Ostentatious describes someone or something that displays wealth, knowledge, power, etc., in a way that is meant to attract attention, admiration, or envy. Things that are ostentatious tend to stand out as overly elaborate or conspicuous.

    The resort town is famous for its extravagantly expensive summer homes, which some tourists view as impressive and others merely ostentatious.

    2 ductile

    [ ˈdəktəl ]
    (adj.) capable of being drawn out into wire or thread; easily led or influenced; capable of being fashioned into a new form.

    The web-like blue and gold dial is housed in a 40mm case made of tantalum, a very hard, ductile, lustrous, blue-gray transition metal that is highly corrosion-resistant.
    例句中的 dail 在这里的含义是表盘。

    3 synonym

    [ ˈsinəˌnim ]
    (n.) one of two or more words or expressions of the same language that have the same or nearly the same meaning in some or all senses.

    The debris hurtled by so fast that the New York Times editorial page seemed to run out of synonyms for disgust, revulsion and abuse.

    4 corrosion

    [ kəˈrōzhən ]
    (n.) the action, process, or effect of corroding

    The corrosion of family values that is often brought on by great wealth.

    5 lustrous

    [ ˈləstrəs ]
    (adj.) reflecting light evenly and efficiently without glitter or sparkle

    The lustrous finish on the satin bedspread adds to the feeling of luxury.

    6 debris

    [ dəˈbrē ]
    (n.) the remains of something broken down or destroyed.

    After the earthquake, rescuers began digging through the debris in search of survivors.

    7 extravagantly

    [ ikˈstravigənt ]
    (adj.) exceeding the limits of reason or necessity

    The company has been making extravagant claims about the drug's effectiveness.

    8 conspicuous

    [ kənˈspikyəwəs ]
    (adj.) obvious to the eye or mind

    There were a number of conspicuous changes to the building.

    9 prominent

    [ ˈprämənənt ]
    (adj.) standing out or projecting beyond a surface or line

    He quickly became prominent in the music industry.

    10 leverage

    [ ˈlevərij ]
    (n.) the action of a lever or the mechanical advantage gained by it
    (vt.) to provide (something, such as a corporation) or supplement (something, such as money) with leverage

    The union's size gave it leverage in the labor contract negotiations.
    The company wants to leverage its brands more effectively.

    1 palatable

    [ ˈpælətəbl ]
    (adj.) (of food or drink) pleasant to taste.
    This is a very palatable local red wine.

    2 ounce

    [ ouns ]
    (n.) a unit of weight of one sixteenth of a pound avoirdupois (approximately 28 grams).
    Melt three ounces of butter in a large frying pan.

    3 naringin

    [ nəˈrinjə̇n ]
    柚苷，柚皮苷
    (n.) Naringin is a flavonoid, specifically a flavanone glycoside, found in citrus fruits like grapefruit, and known for its bitter taste.

    4 antioxidant

    [ ˌæntiˈɒksɪdənt ]
    (n.) a substance that inhibits oxidation, especially one used to counteract the deterioration of stored food products
    An antioxidant is a substance that prevents or slow down oxidation, a chemical reaction that produces harmful free radicals.

    5 arteriosclerosis

    [ ɑ:ˌtɪəriəʊskləˈrəʊsɪs ]
    (n.) Arteriosclerosis is a type of vascular disease where the blood vessels carrying oxygen away from the heart (arteries) become damaged from factors such as high cholesterol, high blood pressure, diabetes and certain genetic influences.

    6 miniature

    [ ˈmɪnətʃə(r) ]
    (adj.) (especially of a replica of something) of a much smaller size than normal; very small.
    Children dressed as miniature adults.

    (n.) a thing that is much smaller than normal, especially a small replica or model.
    Seven full-size car bodies and three miniatures were used.

    (v.) represent on a smaller scale; reduce to miniature dimensions.
    She saw her own reflection miniatured.

    7 cleanse

    [ klenz ]
    (vt.) to rid of impurities by or as if by washing
    It's that mystical state most athletes are lucky to achieve a few times in their careers. The mind is cleansed. The body is free.

    8 citrus

    [ ˈsi-trəs ]
    (n.) any of a group of often thorny trees and shrubs (Citrus and related genera) of the rue family grown in warm regions for their edible fruit (such as the orange or lemon) with firm usually thick rind and pulpy flesh.
    Go back in time and connect with nature at Neapolis Archeological Park, which has an ancient Greek theater, Roman amphitheater, and centuries-old citrus trees.

    9 cruciferous

    [ /ˈkrüsəfər / ]
    (n.) any of a family (Brassicaceae synonym Cruciferae) of plants including the cabbage, turnip, and mustard

    brussel sprout 芽球甘蓝
